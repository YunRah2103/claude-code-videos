# How the car was made — SUMMARY

Vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps, 64 s. Cinematic low-poly 3D (glow, light rays, macro mechanism shots, big centred one-word captions).
ONE car sits on a glowing studio turntable and gains one real invention per stage. A coloured scan plane "upgrades" it each time:
Benz tricycle → hand-crank car → Cadillac electric starter → Ford Model T → Volvo three-point belt → Oldsmobile microprocessor → today's Tesla.
Main character: Yub (3D, from `characters/yub-3d/`).

## On-screen hook (from frame 1)
**THE FIRST CAR HAD LESS THAN 1 HORSEPOWER**

## Full narration (one Gemini TTS call, voice "Charon"; first word at 0.30 s)
1. The first car had less than one horsepower. Here's how it became this.
2. 1886. Karl Benz patents the Benz Patent-Motorwagen.
3. Three wheels, one cylinder, and 0.75 horsepower. That's three quarters of a horse.
4. You steered it with a tiller. Top speed? About 10 miles an hour. Still too fast for me.
5. And early cars had to be hand-cranked. Dangerously.
6. So in 1912, Cadillac brought in Charles Kettering's electric starter. Click. Done.
7. Then Ford's Model T launched at $850.
8. The moving assembly line cut build time from 12.5 hours to 93 minutes.
9. Over 15 million were built, and the price fell to $260. Finally, a car in my budget.
10. 1959. Volvo engineer Nils Bohlin perfects the three-point seatbelt, and Volvo gives the patent away, free.
11. Finally, something holding me together.
12. 1977. The Oldsmobile Toronado is the first car with a microprocessor.
13. Then the code kept coming.
14. Today, a car can have hundreds of millions of lines of code. A Boeing 737? About 15 million.
15. And I still can't pair my phone.
16. Now even features are software. In 2019, Tesla sold an Acceleration Boost for $2,000. My horsepower has a paywall.
17. Which of these was the biggest upgrade?
