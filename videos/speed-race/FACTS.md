# Speed race: fact check

Script: "Could you outrun an F1 car? A top sprinter: about 45 kilometres an hour. A cheetah: roughly double.
A bullet train: over 300. An F1 car: can top 350. You lose. Who would you race?"

| Claim (on screen) | Figure used | Evidence | Wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top sprinter: about 45 km/h (counter/bar **45**) | ~44.7 km/h (12.4 m/s) peak | Usain Bolt's 9.58 s 100 m, Berlin 2009 World Championships; peak instantaneous speed about 12.3–12.4 m/s in published speed analyses of the race (secondary write-ups: [ScienceABC](https://www.scienceabc.com/sports/whats-the-fastest-a-human-can-run.html), [Science News Explores](https://www.snexplores.org/article/olympics-records-how-fast-can-humans-go)) | "about 45"; this is the fastest human ever measured, not a typical sprinter |
| Cheetah: roughly double (**93**) | 93 km/h (25.9 m/s) | Wilson et al. 2013, *Nature* 498:185, "Locomotion dynamics of hunting in wild cheetahs" (GPS/IMU collars, 367 runs, Botswana); [ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/06/130614082900.htm), [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/130612-cheetah-speed-hunting-big-cats-animal-science) | "roughly double" (93 / 45 ≈ 2.1); older anecdotal 110+ km/h figures are not used |
| Bullet train: over 300 (**320**) | 320 km/h commercial top speed | JR East E5 series Shinkansen, raised to 320 km/h on the Tohoku Shinkansen in March 2013 ([Railway Gazette](https://www.railwaygazette.com/passenger/jr-east-introduces-320-km/h-running/37860.article), [JR East press release](https://www.jreast.co.jp/e/press/20101101/)) | "over 300"; faster test/record runs exist but are not service speeds |
| F1 car: can top 350 (**350+**) | 372.5 km/h race-weekend speed trap | Valtteri Bottas, Williams FW38, 2016 Mexican Grand Prix; [Williams F1](https://www.williamsf1.com/posts/253b1dc5-aee9-4d3b-b720-8e30f3a83093/how-fast-can-an-f1-car-go), [Formula1.com stats](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/features/2016/10/f1-mexico-gp-stats-hamilton-prost.html) | "can top 350": shown as 350+, which stays below the record; typical race top speeds vary by track |

## What was softened

- The sprinter figure is the human record peak (Bolt), so the line says "a top sprinter: about 45". It doesn't claim a typical runner.
- The cheetah uses the lower, measured wild figure (93 km/h). The line doesn't use the popular 110–120 km/h anecdote.
- For the train, the line says "over 300" and the chart shows 320, the E5's service speed. Test-run records aren't used.
- For F1, the line says "can top 350" and the chart shows "350+", not the 372.5 km/h peak. Unofficial runs (Bonneville 2006, 397 km/h) aren't used.
- The bar chart is drawn to scale with these four numbers. The poll is two buttons with no vote counts, so no audience data is made up.
- No logos, liveries or real brand marks appear: the F1 car is plain red with a white stripe, and the train is a generic white and blue design.
