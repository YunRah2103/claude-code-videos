# seatbelt-fight — fact check (2026-11-08)

| Line in script | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Your seatbelt can tighten by itself" / pretensioners exist and tighten the belt in a crash | Verified. NHTSA: pretensioners retract the belt to remove slack almost instantly when the car senses a crash. | NHTSA DOT HS 811 835 (Kahane, 2013) |
| "a small charge or motor" | Verified with nuance. Most crash pretensioners fire a small pyrotechnic gas generator (NHTSA; SAE USCAR48 covers pyrotechnic pretensioners). Electric motor (reversible) retractors exist (ZF ACR8, Joyson motorized seatbelt) but mainly tighten the belt *before / around* a likely crash and can release again. "can" kept as the hedge. | NHTSA 811 835; SAE USCAR48; ZF ACR8 product page; Joyson Safety Systems motorized seatbelt page |
| "pull it snug in a fraction of a second" | Fair, hedged with "can". Pyro units act within milliseconds (industry/repair sources quote ~15–30 ms; not a regulatory figure); motor units are slower but still well under a second. No number is shown on screen. | NHTSA 811 835 ("almost instantly"); Autoliv seatbelt technology page |
| "Then it eases off a little so it doesn't crush your chest" | Verified. Load (force) limiters let the belt yield/pay out once belt force passes a set level, to lower chest loads; NHTSA estimates pretensioner + load limiter cut belted front-occupant fatality risk by ~12.8 %. Kept as "a little". | NHTSA 811 835; Autoliv seatbelt page |
| "Did you know that?" | Question, no claim. | - |
