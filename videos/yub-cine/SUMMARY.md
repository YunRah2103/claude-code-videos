# Yub Cars (@yub_cars): three cinematic low-poly shorts

The three shorts are `ioniq_fake_gears.mp4`, `gm_carplay.mp4` and `jeep_wave.mp4`. They are vertical (1080x1920), 30 fps, and 32–34 s long.

**Look.** The style follows the matrix-headlight reference. Scenes are smooth-shaded low-poly 3D with soft sun shadows, ambient occlusion, glossy paint, and bloom on every light. They also use light beams, depth-of-field macro shots, and rich saturated colour: a night race track, a blue-hour dealership and a golden-hour desert.

**Text.** A big hook line is on screen from the first frame and is spoken, with the first word at 0.30 s. Captions are one bold word at a time, centred in a fixed position. Every frame was checked by script for clipped or overlapping overlay text, and none was found.

**Main character (Yub).** He has brown skin, silver square glasses, a middle-part fringe, a black cap with a white "LTN" logo, a plain white T-shirt, black jeans and white sneakers. He is silent, and the jokes come from his reactions.

**Voice.** There is one narrator: Gemini TTS, voice "Charon". It was called once per video, and no 429 error occurred.

**Music.** All three use "Hot Swing" by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), licensed CC BY 4.0. A synthesised rock backbeat is layered on top, locked to the track's 144 BPM. The music sits about 3.5 dB under the voice and stays clearly audible throughout.

But Cars.com says nearly nine in ten shoppers value smartphone integration, and forty-six percent call it a must-have. Nearly half of shoppers say they must have it. GM says: how about no. Would you buy a car without CarPlay? Yes, or no?

## 3. jeep_wave.mp4 (~32 s)

**Final narration**
> Jeep drivers have a secret rule. Pass another Jeep, and the driver waves at you. It's called the Jeep Wave, a tradition between Jeep drivers. It's believed to go back to World War Two, when soldiers drove the original Willys Jeeps. There are no official rules. But the unwritten one is simple: if a fellow Jeep driver waves, you wave back. Wave at a different brand? Nothing. Jeep later turned it into an official owner program, called Jeep Wave. A wave so popular, the company put a name on it. Should every car brand have a wave? Yes, or no?
