# Yub low-poly shorts: Toyota key fob, Tesla Acceleration Boost, Veyron tyres

Three vertical shorts for @yub_cars in the same PS1 low-poly narrated style as `rolls_royce_pinstripe.mp4` and
`ferrari_purrari.mp4`. Each one is 1080x1920 at 30 fps and about 24.5 s long (25 s maximum). Each has one narrator
(Gemini TTS voice Charon, one call per video, no 429s), 16 or 17 literal shots (a new picture about every 1.5 s) and
small word-by-word captions. Nothing is drawn over the picture in the first 3 s: captions start at 3.0 s. The first
spoken word starts at about 0.33 s. There are no thumbnails. Facts were checked on 2026-11-24.

Background music, the same track in all three: "Hep Cats" by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), licensed under
Creative Commons BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). It sits about 24 dB under the voice and dips
a further ~5 dB while the narrator speaks. **Attribution required** in each upload's description:
`Music: "Hep Cats" Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), Licensed under Creative Commons: By Attribution 4.0`.
The brief left the background track path as "[PATH]", so this track was chosen as the jazz bed you asked for.

## 1. toyota_key_fob.mp4

### Final narration (87 words)
> Toyota can stop your own key from starting your own car.
> On many 2018-and-newer Toyotas, remote start from the key fob needs a paid subscription.
> When it launched, it cost eight dollars a month, or eighty a year.
> The fob sends a radio signal, but the car's software checks that you paid.
> After the backlash, Toyota said it wasn't intentional, and that it was reviewing it.
> You own the key. You rent the button.
> Should a button on your own key cost a subscription? Yes, or no?

### Facts used
| # | Fact (as used) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A Toyota spokesperson confirmed that a 2018-or-later Toyota with Remote Connect "must be enrolled in a valid subscription in order for the key fob to start the car remotely". | The Drive, "Toyota Made Its Key Fob Remote Start Into a Subscription Service" (2021-12-09): https://www.thedrive.com/news/43329/toyota-made-its-key-fob-remote-start-into-a-subscription-service |
| 2 | It applies to 2018 and later models, with a free Remote Connect trial of 3 to 10 years. The narration says "many 2018-and-newer Toyotas", not every model. | Engadget, "Toyota's remote start key fob feature requires an $8 monthly subscription" (2021-12-14): https://www.engadget.com/toyota-key-fob-remote-start-function-requires-subscription-111606852.html |
| 3 | At the time it cost $8 a month or $80 a year. The narration says "when it launched"; no current price is given. | The Drive (above): "either $8 a month or $80 a year at the Remote Connect plan's current price"; Engadget (above) |
| 4 | The fob talks to the car by radio and needs no connection to Toyota's servers. The car (not the fob) enforces the subscription. | The Drive (above): "Your fob uses radio waves to communicate with the car, and no connection back to Toyota's servers is needed." |
| 5 | After the backlash, Toyota said it "wasn't intentional" and that it was reviewing it. | The Drive, "Toyota 'Reviewing' Key Fob Remote Start Subscription Plan After Massive Blowback" (2021-12-23): https://www.thedrive.com/tech/43636/toyota-reviewing-key-fob-remote-start-subscription-plan-after-massive-blowback |

**Changed from the brief:** the brief has Toyota calling it "unintentional". The Drive uses that word in its own
summary, but Toyota's words in the article are that it "wasn't intentional". The narration uses Toyota's wording.
"Reviewing" comes from The Drive's headline. The article body says the executive team is "examining whether it's
possible to reverse course".

> It's an over-the-air software upgrade that cut about half a second from zero to sixty.
> A month earlier, Tesla had given every Model 3 a smaller power boost, for free.
> Free update first. Paid update second.
> Would you pay $2,000 for half a second? Yes, or no?

### Facts used

## 3. veyron_tires.mp4

### Final narration (75 words)
> A set of Bugatti Veyron tires used to cost $42,000.
> Each one was a bespoke Michelin run-flat, glued to the wheel.
> With the wheels, a full replacement could reach about $100,000.
> Now Bugatti has developed larger wheels that fit modern tires, costing around $3,600 a set.
> The catch: owners still have to pay for the new wheels.
> Cheaper tires. Just not a cheap fix.
> Would you pay $100,000 to change your tires? Yes, or no?

### Facts used
| # | Fact (as used) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A set of Veyron tyres cost up to $42,000 to replace, or about $100,000 with wheels. | Motor1, "Michelin Reinforced 300-MPH Bugatti Chiron Tires With Carbon Fiber" (2019-09-13): https://www.motor1.com/news/370799/michelin-bugatti-chiron-300-mph-carbon-fiber/ ("cost up to $42,000 to replace (or $100,000 with wheels)") |
| 2 | They are bespoke Michelin Pilot Sport PAX run-flat tyres, glued (bonded) to the wheel. | Motor1, "Find Out Why The Bugatti Veyron's Tires Cost $42,000 Per Set" (2020-10-14): https://www.motor1.com/news/448940/bugatti-veyron-tire-replacement-cost/ ("Michelin Pilot Sport PAX tires that are bespoke for the Bugatti"; the adhesive and the polyurethane-and-rubber run-flat insert) |
| 3 | Bugatti (La Maison Pur Sang) has developed new, larger factory wheels (20 in front, 21 in rear) that take Chiron-size modern tyres. | Bugatti Newsroom press release (2026-08-20): https://newsroom.bugatti.com/press-releases/la-maison-pur-sang-one-of-one-restored-veyron ("a new wheel concept that enables the Veyron to use tires in Chiron dimensions") |
| 4 | The modern tyres cost about $3,600 a set, but owners must also pay for the new wheels. | Carscoops, "Even Veyron Owners Balk At $42,000 Tires, But Bugatti's $3,600 Fix Has A Catch" (Aug 2026): https://www.carscoops.com/2026/08/bugatti-veyron-wheels-tires/ ; also Jalopnik (2026-08-20): https://www.jalopnik.com/2241249/bugatti-veyron-recommission-larger-oem-wheels-dont-need-pax-tires/ |

**Changed from the brief:**
- "Each Veyron tyre was built by hand": **left out.** No source I could read says the tyres were hand-built. Motor1
  calls them "bespoke" and explains the cost through the PAX run-flat design and the glue. The narration uses that
  instead: "a bespoke Michelin run-flat, glued to the wheel".
- "More than 20 years after the car launched": **left out.** None of the sources I read gives the Veyron's launch
  year, so I could not check the gap. The narration says "Now" (the new wheels were announced in August 2026).
- The brief's Carscoops link (`?p=3486997`) opened the homepage, so the full article URL is listed above.
- The ~$3,600 is a retail price for comparable modern tyres in those sizes, as reported by Carscoops and Jalopnik.
  It is not an official Bugatti price. The narration says "around $3,600" and gives no price for the wheels.
- No Chiron tyre price is stated or shown.

**Oddity (never mentioned):** a pink flamingo stands on one leg on top of a tyre stack at the back of the tyre shop
(hook, wide shop shots and the closing question).
