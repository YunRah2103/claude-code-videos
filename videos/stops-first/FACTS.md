# Stops first: fact check

Script: "Which hypercar stops first? Same test: zero to 400 kilometres an hour, then back to zero. Bugatti's Chiron
set the record at about 42 seconds. Years later, Rimac's Nevera did it in about 30. Twelve seconds quicker. In a race,
the Nevera would already be parked. Would you take the electric one? Yes or no?"

| Claim | Figure used | Source | Wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chiron 0-400-0 km/h | 41.96 s (clock freeze **41.96 s**) | Bugatti press release, September 2017 (record run with Juan Pablo Montoya) | "about 42 seconds" |
| Nevera 0-400-0 km/h | 29.93 s (clock freeze **29.93 s**) | Rimac press release on the Nevera's 0-400-0 km/h record | "about 30" |
| Gap | 41.96 - 29.93 = 12.03 s (marker **12 s**) | derived | "Twelve seconds quicker" |

## What was softened / how it is shown

- The voice uses hedges ("about 42", "about 30"); the timeline ticks read "≈42 s" and "≈30 s". Only the final clock
  readings are data.
- The two runs were separate record attempts, years apart, on different days and tracks. The caption
  "separate record runs" stays on screen while the clocks run. "In a race, the Nevera would already be parked" is a
  hypothetical comparison of the two published times, not a head-to-head result.
- Intermediate clock and speed readings are interpolated for the animation: each clock gives 75% of its run to
  accelerating and 25% to braking, and time runs compressed while accelerating (shown by the ▶▶ mark). The real
  accel/brake splits are not claimed.
- The cars are 3D models built in Blender (bpy 4.2) to look close to the real cars, at the user's request. They carry no
  logos, badges or brand artwork; the names appear only as text labels.
- Shape references: the Chiron's side, top and front silhouettes were traced from the free 2016 Bugatti Chiron
  blueprint on getoutlines.com (CC BY 4.0, https://getoutlines.com/blueprints/23142/2016-bugatti-chiron-coupe-blueprints).
  The Nevera was modelled from its published dimensions (4.750 x 1.986 x 1.208 m, 2.745 m wheelbase) and Creative
  Commons photos on Wikimedia Commons ("Rimac - Nevera 01-10", "2023 Rimac Nevera" and others), because no free
  blueprint was available. The reference images are not shown in the video.
